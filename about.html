About

Faster typing was never the point.

CODEWORM started from a simple observation about what programming education has been optimising for.

Why CODEWORM exists

We spent years teaching the part that's now being automated.

For years we taught developers to write code. For years we built software ourselves. Both taught us the same thing: the hard part was never the syntax.

Then AI began writing code, applications, tests and documentation. Much of what programming education sells — how to produce an implementation — is becoming a commodity.

What isn't becoming a commodity is knowing what to build, why, and whether the thing that got built is any good. The next generation of developers needs stronger judgment, not merely faster typing.

CODEWORM Build Lab is our attempt to teach that. We don't claim to have finished working out how.

What we won't do

The tone is part of the product.

  • Promise jobs, or timelines to a job.
  • Manufacture urgency.
  • Claim AI does more — or less — than it does.
  • Sell a framework as if it were a career.
  • Hide the parts that went wrong.

Still researching

CODEWORM is itself an experiment.

We're continuously building, breaking and re-examining our own assumptions — in the labs, and in how we teach. When we change our minds, we'll say so.

How this site is built

The website should practise what it teaches.

Eight decisions, with the reasoning and the cost. If the requirements change, so will these.

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript
WhySeven mostly static pages with no shared runtime state. Nothing needs hydrating, so nothing needs a framework.
CostThe header and footer are repeated in every file. Accepted at seven pages.
No Astro, no React
WhyA build step and a dependency tree would add moving parts without meeting any requirement we have.
Revisit whenField notes and case studies make content, not layout, the main thing we maintain.
JavaScript only as enhancement
WhyThree small scripts handle the menu, tabs and motion. Every page reads and works without them.
CostMotion is switched off for reduced-motion settings, and the smoke drops away on slow GPUs. The site is calmer there, not broken.
Smooth scroll, glass and smoke
WhyScroll-linked motion appears only where it carries meaning: the decision layer clears from smoke as you reach it, and statements light up as you read them. One self-hosted library (Lenis) and one small WebGL shader.
CostAbout 12 KB of gzipped JavaScript and some GPU work the site did not need before. Revisit if it ever hurts reading, Core Web Vitals or battery.
An opt-in reading guide, without a camera
WhyIt underlines the sentence you're probably reading, inferred from where you scroll and from your pointer or touch. It is off until you switch it on, works on-device, and sends nothing anywhere.
CostIt is a guess, not eye tracking: it can be a line off, and it stays quiet while you skim. It needs a browser with the CSS Custom Highlight API; elsewhere the switch simply doesn't appear.
Installable, and readable offline
WhyA manifest and one small service worker: every page works offline after a first visit, and the site can live on a home screen. Content is fetched fresh whenever you're online.
CostA cache to keep honest. The worker has a version constant that must change when files are added or removed. There is still no offline content beyond the site itself.
Contact form opens your email app
WhyNo server, no database, no stored personal data, nothing to secure or breach.
CostNo delivery confirmation, and it depends on the visitor having an email client. Revisit if volume justifies a backend.
Two typefaces, self-hosted
WhyA grotesk for display and Manrope for everything else carry the identity without decoration. Serving them from this site means no third-party request, no render-blocking font stylesheet, and type that works offline.
CostAbout 47 KB of font files we now carry ourselves, one Latin subset each. Other scripts fall back to system fonts.
