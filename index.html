The decision layer

The code is not the interesting part.

Ask AI for an authentication system and you'll get one. Engineering judgment starts with what the code doesn't say. Open a question.

01Why this authentication model?

Sessions, tokens and delegated identity each move risk somewhere different. Choosing one is choosing where the failure will live.

02Where are credentials stored?

Storage decides what an attacker gets after a breach — and what you can prove afterwards.

03What is the threat model?

Without one, "secure" has no meaning. Who attacks, for what, with what access?

04What happens when verification fails?

Error messages leak. Retries invite abuse. Failure behaviour is a design decision, not an exception handler.

05How are sessions invalidated?

Logout, password change, stolen device. What you can't revoke, you can't recover from.

06What happens during abuse?

Credential stuffing looks like normal traffic until you decide what abnormal means.

07What assumptions does the architecture make?

Every design assumes something about users, networks and time. Most failures live in the assumption nobody wrote down.

08What happens when traffic increases?

Password hashing, session stores and rate limits all behave differently at 10×. Which one breaks first?

09How will it be monitored?

If you can't see a pattern of failed logins, it isn't a control. It's a hope.
