How every experiment is written

Eight fields. The reasoning stays on the page.

A lab isn't a walkthrough. It's a record of what we decided, what happened, and what we'd do differently.

  1. 01Problem

    What we set out to solve, and under which constraints.

  2. 02Decision

    What we chose.

  3. 03Reason

    Why — and what we rejected.

  4. 04Implementation

    What we built, briefly.

  5. 05Failure

    Where it broke, or where we broke it.

  6. 06Investigation

    How we found the real cause.

  7. 07Result

    What we measured or observed.

  8. 08Lesson

    What transfers to the next system.

Preview — LAB 001

Click a failure to open the investigation.

These are the hypotheses we'll test in the authentication lab. They are questions, not findings.

FailureA session survives a password change
Hypothesis
Sessions are issued once and never re-checked against credential changes.
Investigation
Change the password in one browser. Try to keep using the old session in another.
Decision at stake
Where session state lives, and what can revoke it.
FailureLogin errors reveal which accounts exist
Hypothesis
Different responses for "unknown user" and "wrong password" let an attacker enumerate accounts.
Investigation
Compare responses, status codes and timing for both cases.
Decision at stake
How much the system tells an unauthenticated stranger.
FailureNothing notices a credential-stuffing run
Hypothesis
Thousands of failed logins from many addresses look like ordinary traffic to a system without detection.
Investigation
Replay a synthetic run and check what the system logs, limits and reports.
Decision at stake
What "abnormal" means, and who finds out.
